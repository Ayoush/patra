# State Aliases and Historical Context

This document lists historical aliases of Indian states alongside
their modern canonical names, with the reason and year for each change.

| Alias | Canonical     | Why / When |
| :--- |:--------------| :--- |
| Orissa | Odisha        | Renamed in 2011 by the Orissa (Alteration of Name) Act to reflect the native Odia pronunciation and identity. |
| Pondicherry | Puducherry    | Renamed in 2006 to restore the original Tamil name, dropping the anglicized French colonial spelling. |
| Uttaranchal | Uttarakhand   | Renamed in 2007 to reflect the traditional Sanskrit name meaning "Northern Land." |
| Bombay | Maharashtra   | The state of Maharashtra was formed in 1960 on linguistic lines; "Bombay" referred to the city, which was renamed Mumbai in 1995. |
| Madras | Tamil Nadu    | Renamed in 1969 to reflect Tamil linguistic and cultural identity rather than the colonial name of its capital. |
| Mysore | Karnataka     | Renamed in 1973 to reflect the Kannada-speaking majority, moving away from the name of the former princely state. |
| Calcutta | Kolkata       | Renamed in 2001 to align with the Bengali pronunciation and drop the British anglicized spelling. |
| United Provinces | Uttar Pradesh | Renamed in 1950 upon India's independence; "Uttar Pradesh" means "Northern Province" in Hindi. |
| Rajputana | Rajasthan     | Renamed in 1949 when the princely states merged into the Republic of India; means "Land of Kings." |
| East Punjab | Punjab        | Renamed simply to Punjab in 1966 after the Punjab Reorganisation Act split the state. |
| Coorg | Kodagu        | Renamed in 1973 to reflect the native Kannada name of the district. |
